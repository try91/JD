**Brief introduction**

In this study, the discovery analyses were conducted in two community-based cohorts from Jiading District, Shanghai, China. The Jiading (JD)_2010 cohort comprised 10,327 adults aged 40–93 years who were enrolled in 2010, of whom 4,464 participants were randomly selected to form the JD_sub cohort and underwent fecal shotgun metagenomic profiling in 2014. Baseline characteristics of JD_sub participants in 2010 were comparable to those of the overall JD_2010 cohort. Demographic, lifestyle, medication, and clinical data were collected at the 2010 baseline examination and the 2014 follow-up visit. Incident diabetes, chronic kidney disease (CKD), and cardiovascular disease (CVD) were first ascertained in 2014. For CVD, the follow-up was further extended to 2021, with a median follow-up period of 11.1 years (interquartile range [IQR]: 10.9–11.2 for JD_2010; 11.0–11.2 for JD_sub) from the 2010 baseline.

Using serum samples collected in 2010, we quantified 19 EDCs or their metabolites across four chemical classes: per- and polyfluoroalkyl substances (PFAS), phthalate esters (PAEs), antimicrobials, and bisphenols. In JD_2010, we examined the associations of EDC analytes—both individually and as mixtures—with 48 cardiometabolic and kidney biomarkers spanning 10 categories, nine prevalent disorders, and incident diabetes, CKD, and CVD. In JD_sub, we profiled gut microbiota composition and assessed its associations with EDC analytes and with incident diabetes, CKD, and CVD. Building on these EDC–disease and microbiome–disease analyses and integrating multiple external cohorts and sensitivity analyses, we further evaluated the interactive effects between EDCs and gut microbial species on the risks of diabetes, CKD, and CVD.

**Data availability**: Metagenomic sequencing data of the 4,464 fecal DNA samples from the JD_sub cohort have been deposited at the China Nucleotide Sequence Archive (CNSA) under accession no. CNP0004479 for controlled access (https://doi.org/10.26036/CNP0004479). Access requests for the raw sequencing data are reviewed by the Data Access Committee of Ruijin Hospital, Shanghai Jiao Tong University School of Medicine; inquiries should be sent to tiange.wang@shsmu.edu.cn. Individual-level de-identified clinical phenotypes, EDC analyte measurements, gut microbial composition profiles, and functional pathway abundances used in the main analyses have been deposited at the ChinaMAP database (http://www.mbiobank.com/research/4c/jd/edc_microbiome).
